      How to prevent tick bites

      When you're outdoors

      • Stick to paths and avoid long grass and dense undergrowth.
      • Wear long sleeves and tuck your trousers into your socks.
      • Wear light-coloured clothing so ticks are easier to spot.
      • Use an insect repellent that works against ticks (for example, one containing DEET).

      After a walk

      • Check your skin, especially armpits, groin, waist and behind the knees.
      • Check children's heads and necks, including the hairline.
      • Brush off clothing before going indoors.

      Protecting your pets

      • Check your dog or cat after every walk: around the ears, face, armpits and between the toes.
      • Ask your vet about a tick prevention treatment.
      • Keep long grass in your garden short.

      If you find a tick

      • Remove it as soon as possible with fine-tipped tweezers or a tick removal tool.
      • Grip it as close to the skin as you can and pull upwards slowly and steadily.
      • Don't squeeze, burn or cover it in creams.
      • Clean the bite and watch it for a few weeks. See a GP if you get a spreading rash or flu-like symptoms.

      General guidance only, based on NHS advice. For medical help see a GP; for pets, speak to your vet. NHS: Lyme disease
